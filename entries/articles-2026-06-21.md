## 三层次
- meaning: 一篇关于人生意识三个层次的思考：生存、觉醒、宇宙视角，以及如何在宏大视角和现实生活之间保持平衡。
- 原文:
  I think there are three levels of awareness about life.
  At the first level, people are absorbed by daily survival: food, rent, bills, money, and practical responsibilities.
  At the second level, some people begin to realize that life is limited, and that life is a journey to be experienced rather than something to simply get through.
  At the final level, we see ourselves from a cosmic perspective. Human beings are ultimately dust, just a brief moment in the universe. From that view, life and death may matter less than we usually imagine. They are part of our destiny.
  GPT reply:
  I think your idea is quite deep, and the structure is strong.
  The first level is survival. Most people spend much of their energy dealing with pressure: money, housing, food, family, work, health. This level may look ordinary, but it is real. When people are under pressure, philosophy feels far away.
  The second level is awakening. A person starts to realize, "My life is limited. I should actually experience it." This is where people begin to care about freedom, travel, learning, love, beauty, self-development, and meaning.
  The third level is the cosmic view. From the universe's perspective, human life is extremely small. We appear, we struggle, we love, we suffer, and then we disappear. This thought can feel sad, but it can also be freeing. If everything is temporary, then many fears, comparisons, and social pressures become smaller.
  My view is this: the third level should give you peace, but it should not make you passive. Even if life is tiny on a cosmic scale, your pain, your family, your daughter, your effort, and your choices still matter at the human scale.
  A balanced sentence could be:
  We are tiny in the universe, but our experience is still real to us.
  That is probably the healthiest version of your idea.

## 无社交媒体生活
- meaning: 一篇关于摆脱社交媒体、重新找回注意力、真实连接和内心平静的演讲稿。
- 原文:
  Let me ask you a question: what's most likely the very first thing you're going to do when you find yourself bored? Is it going outside for a run? Is it spending time on some hobbies? Is it working on your goals and aspirations? Well, it most likely looks something along the lines like this.
  In this day and age, it almost feels as if social media is necessary in navigating the current years of our lives. Yet, it almost feels as if we're constantly screwing away the most important years of our lives instead. So let me ask you a question, a question that might sound ridiculous at first: if you had the chance to live a life completely free and away from social media, would you?
  At the start of high school, I got my first ever phone, an iPhone SE, and I was super hyped to finally be able to play Geometry Dash and Clash of Clans whenever and wherever I wanted. But in high school, games like these were no longer the hottest thing out there anymore, as there's this new thing that everyone had started to use: Facebook, Snapchat, Instagram-social media. And since everyone else was using them, it kind of felt like I had to use them too, so I installed them.
  And I can't even lie, those times back then were actually pretty fun. It was pretty fun to be able to talk to my friends whenever and wherever I wanted, to follow people who shared the same singular brain cell as me, and I honestly felt more connected than ever before.
  But while all this was happening, there was unfortunately a very bad side effect that began to take place. I don't exactly remember when, but I suddenly developed a habit of endlessly scrolling on my phone. Whenever I woke up, I would grab my phone and scroll. Whenever I got home, I would scroll. Whenever there was any sort of empty space where absolutely nothing was happening at all, I would grab my phone and scroll. And this type of scroll wasn't doing anything: I wasn't learning, excited, or interested. I was just mindlessly wasting hours of my time on other people's lives.
  From the outside looking in, it was clearly a problem, but since everyone else was doing it, I really didn't think much of it at all and thought it was completely normal. But this pattern of behavior spiraled into something even worse in the years that followed.
  After graduating and having a really depressing first year in university, I was completely lost with what the hell I was going to do with my life and ended up taking a year off. Now imagine that suddenly, instead of having classes, studying, or meeting up with others, you had all the free time in the world. What would you do with it?
  Well, for me, it didn't change; the scrolling had gotten even worse as I was constantly on my phone to distract myself from all the problems I've actively tried to avoid, and started to feel sluggish, tired, and unmotivated to do absolutely anything.
  Obviously, the scrolling itself was already bad, but the stuff I was scrolling-looking at all these people sharing their extravagant lives online-what does that even mentally do to someone? I started constantly comparing my life with others: "Hey look, someone's partying in university, this person's in a perfect relationship, this person's traveling across the world, this person looks as if they have everything all figured out," while I was in my bed scrolling at 1:00 in the morning feeling absolutely worthless.
  I knew this was a problem, but my identity, friendships, almost everything was deeply rooted on social media, and it felt impossible to leave it all behind. But as the days kept blurring away, I finally realized that I couldn't keep repeating the cycle and needed to make a change. Looking at myself in the mirror, I finally made the decision that, "You know what? This isn't worth it anymore. I'm done."
  The first few weeks without social media were whack, I can't even lie. All of a sudden, I had time-way too much time. But now I was completely bored out of my mind since I no longer got that simple fix through scrolling anymore.
  But after a while, I realized that being bored is actually a good thing, where curiosity, imagination, thinking, and reflection can all happen. And with all that time now, I guess I finally had to deal with all those questions I've actively tried to avoid head-on, search for meaning, and ultimately find things to do. As a result, I discovered new hobbies, new passions, new goals to achieve, reignited old hobbies, and learned a lot about myself, about who I am, and about what I wanted to do in the future.
  For the first time in years, I finally got around to doing a lot of the things I said I was going to do. I finally had energy and felt motivated to do things, and it honestly felt like I was finally participating in this game called life. And honestly, all of this was possible because I was no longer distracted. My attention span was finally fixed to where I could finally sit down and think, finally work on something without constantly grabbing my phone, finally find new things to do whenever I felt bored. I finally felt alive.
  Another thing I was worried about when I first deleted social media was FOMO: the fear of missing out. I was afraid that if I deleted social media, I would miss out on important things and be completely out of the loop on what was happening with my friends, others, and everything around the world. And I definitely felt that.
  But then hours would pass, days would pass, weeks, months... and I finally realized I was missing out on what? Stupid trends, some celebrities, other people's lives. Like, these pieces of information were totally irrelevant and literally did not change or affect my life in any single way. So when facing FOMO, I guess it's important to remember that every second we spend on social media is also another second we miss out on actual, real life.
  Another thing that also worried me was all those people I would lose contact with. But after deleting social media, it turns out that out of all those 500 or something followers, the truth is that most of them weren't really my friends and didn't even know I deleted social media in the first place. Liking and commenting on someone's post or story doesn't make you friends. Your real friends will always have your back and find a way to maintain that connection with you, regardless of if you had social media or not. And even though I might not be as updated anymore, those bonds I share with my friends whenever I see them honestly feel stronger, more genuine than ever. After all, true connection isn't formed through a screen, but through reality.
  But by far the biggest benefit I felt from deleting social media has been my mental health. My life now sometimes genuinely feels like a character straight out of a Ghibli movie, just by simply enjoying all the little things in life, from simply taking walks outside, to eating breakfast by the balcony, to late conversations with friends. Like, every small thing just feels so real and enjoyable now. And instead of constantly sharing every single thing that happens in my life, I can finally now just live in the present and just enjoy the moment, giving this feeling of peace and tranquility like never before.
  Also, as a whole, I just feel genuinely happier now without it. And part of that could be because I was no longer constantly exposed to other people's lives; now I wasn't comparing myself with others anymore. And honestly, now that I think of it, I realized how most of what's on social media is legitimately fake. Like, I remember there was this one time where my friends posted something seemingly having the time of their lives out there, but then messaged me how sick, dead, and bored they were the entire day. Like, what you see on social media ain't reality, bro.
  With all this nonsense gone, my mind finally feels way more at ease now. Those anxious feelings I had are no longer there, and instead of focusing on others, I could finally use that energy to finally focus on my own life, while accepting and being grateful for what I already have, instead of comparing and wishing that I had something else. After all, comparison is the thief of joy.
  Even though social media definitely has its upsides, the way we use it most likely just isn't healthy. It used to be this fun thing to connect with others, but now it seems like it has taken away our time, motivation, interests, mental health, and the social essence of how real human connection is formed.
  It has honestly gotten to a point where it seems like nowadays everyone is glued to their phones: kids on their phones instead of playing outside, couples on their phones instead of talking about life, friends on their phones instead of having a good time.
  And unfortunately, it's happening everywhere. Deleting social media has reminded me that there's a lot of life to be lived outside your phone. That simple things like living in the present, pursuing your passions, making memories-those are the things that truly matter in life.
  So if I was to ask you a question, a question that might have sounded ridiculous at first: if you had the chance to live a life completely free and away from social media, would you?
  Well, for me, the answer is yes.
